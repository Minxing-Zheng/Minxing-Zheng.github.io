Overview

Standard conformal sets can waste coverage in low-density regions. ORCA draws samples from a conditional generative model, ranks them using a local density proxy, and learns a radius for each rank. A separate calibration split gives the resulting union of regions finite-sample marginal coverage. The page below shows the geometric idea through an interactive toy example; it is not a reproduction of the paper's benchmark results.

What is exact here, and what is illustrative?

Paper formulation. ORCA ranks K generated samples using average m-nearest-neighbor distances, and learns rank-specific distance quantiles Qᵣ on an exploration split. Stage 2 minimizes Σᵣ Qᵣᵈ subject to exploration coverage ≥ 1 − α. This is an additive volume proxy, counting overlaps; it is not the volume of the union. The paper presents an exact MILP formulation and scalable alternatives.

Calibration. Hold the learned Q vector fixed. On the independent calibration split, use Sᵢ = minᵣ Eᵢ,ᵣ / Qᵣ and the ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉-th score order statistic tα. The final radii are tα Qᵣ.

P{Ytest ∈ C(Xtest)} ≥ 1 − α
C(Xtest) = ∪ᵣ B(Ŷtest,j(r), tα Qᵣ)

This playground. A fixed-input, two-dimensional Gaussian-mixture model is used as both the response distribution and the fitted generator. Each response has a fresh independent K-sample draw, ranked with m = 4. The dominant and compact modes have unequal variances; the spread ratio controls their standard-deviation ratio, so the variance ratio is its square. Warm shading encodes the actual mixture density with a common, monotone contrast enhancement across all panels. Opening plots are a hand-illustrated allocation example, not calibrated experimental output. There are 1,000 exploration instances and 1,000 calibration instances. A greedy search over 12 distance-quantile candidates per rank, followed by pruning, approximates Stage 2; it is not the exact MILP or a reproduction of the paper’s full greedy algorithm. A tiny positive radius floor avoids division by zero. Split-conformal calibration then follows the score and order-statistic rule above.

Scope of the guarantee. Marginal coverage requires exchangeability of calibration and test instances, consistent sampling/ranking, and a radius direction selected independently of calibration data. It does not promise coverage at every input or for every realized generated-sample draw. Approximate optimization affects efficiency, while independent calibration supplies validity. The paper’s highest-density-region limit needs its additional asymptotic assumptions.

Reading the numbers. Held-out percentages are Monte Carlo estimates, rounded to 0.1 percentage points (at 90%, standard error ≈ 0.95 points). Union area is integrated on a grid with the same coordinate scale for all methods; its range includes every displayed ball. Plots share axes and may crop very large balls.

Method summary: Generative Conformal Prediction with Optimized Coverage Allocation, §2–3, Algorithm 1, equations (3)–(10), and Theorem 3.3. PCP means Probabilistic Conformal Prediction. The playground is illustrative and does not report the paper’s benchmark results.
